Before the shutters go up, the Northern Quarter belongs to whoever is awake enough to see it. A street sweeper. A woman walking a dog too big for her. The smell of bread from somewhere unseen. By ten it will be tourists and brunch queues, noise and performance. At seven it is just brick and quiet.

Marcus Aurelius wrote his private notes in the early hours too, before the business of ruling an empire crowded in. There's something in the stillness of an empty street that makes it easier to remember what actually matters: not the queue, not the noise, not being seen, but simply doing the next right thing with attention.

I don't need the city to be impressive. I just need to notice it before it starts performing for anyone else.
