We've built entire industries around the promise of speed, and yet the good things still insist on taking time. Bread. Friendship. A garden. A city's character.

Manchester wasn't built in a rush and it doesn't reward one either. The best of it — the canals, the old mills turned into something new, the way people actually talk to each other in a queue — only shows itself if you slow down enough to notice.

Maybe that's the whole meditation: noticing is a discipline, not an accident.
